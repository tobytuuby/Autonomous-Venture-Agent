# Venture #1: Buyer-validation package

Status: **PREPARED, UNSENT, ZERO RESPONSES**. Nothing below implies verified demand, buyer contact, a sale, or launch authorization.

## Target customer and proposed offer
Independent pottery/ceramics studios with repeated clay, glaze and consumable restocking, without a comprehensive inventory system. The product is the offline Studio Restock Kit at a **hypothetical $12 one-time price**. Free templates and established inventory tools are major substitutes; we have no evidence customers will pay.

## Prioritized prospect segments (research hypotheses, not identified leads)
1. Community ceramics studios selling open-studio memberships
2. Independent pottery teaching studios with recurring classes
3. Small production ceramicists using several glaze and clay inputs
4. Ceramics makerspaces with shared consumables
5. Studio co-ops with rotating supply coordinators
6. Pottery schools with multiple instructors ordering materials
7. Small ceramics workshops holding weekly events
8. Handmade ceramics sellers fulfilling orders from one studio
9. Pottery studios that also sell firing services
10. Independent clay-supply resellers managing demo-studio supplies

Prioritize segments 1, 2 and 5 because ordering responsibility may move between people. These are assumptions—not researched business names, contact details, market sizes or validated needs.

## Research protocol (only after separate authorization to contact)
Aim for 10 conversations; record each one in buyer-responses.csv, preserving negative answers. Ask permission to take notes and separately ask permission for follow-up. Do not record private contact details in the public repository. Do not offer discounts in exchange for favorable feedback. Do not count interview attendance or verbal praise as sales.

### Interview (8–12 minutes)
1. Tell me about the last time you ran short of an essential clay, glaze or studio consumable. What happened?
2. Who decides when to reorder, and who actually places the order?
3. What do you use today to track counts and supplier pack sizes? What does it cost?
4. How often do you count supplies, and how long does that take?
5. Where does the existing process break down? Ask for a concrete example.
6. Would it help to enter stock/inbound/targets and download an offline pack-based order review? Why or why not?
7. Watch the demo. Can you complete one item without help? Note observed difficulty.
8. What is missing, misleading, or unnecessary?
9. Given your current alternative, would you buy this for **$12 once**, yes/no/unsure? What makes you say that?
10. Would you permit a follow-up? Capture consent yes/no only in the public tracker.

### Objections to record rather than dismiss
- 'I already use a free spreadsheet.' Ask which function is not covered; if none, mark NOT DIFFERENTIATED.
- 'I need multi-user/cloud inventory.' Record PRODUCT GAP; do not promise this is included.
- 'Our supplier minimums are complex.' Confirm the worksheet does not model delivery, tax or minimums.
- 'I don't want to pay.' Log declined willingness to pay without arguing.

## Decision after evidence
Do not authorize spending or launch on intention alone. Review the first 10 valid responses. **Continue limited validation** only if >=3 report repeated painful restock problems, >=2 successfully use the demo, and >=1 expresses credible willingness to pay $12. These are deliberately small pilot indicators, **not** statistically meaningful proof of demand. Recommend a paid-market test only with approved payment/outreach authority. No paid orders => sales = 0 and revenue = $0.

If those thresholds fail, shelve or substantially revise the offer; do not spend the $100 venture seed trying to force demand.
